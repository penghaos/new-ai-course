# Layout Patterns

Choose one pattern from the source's information relationship, not from habit. Default to landscape 4:3 at a target canvas of 1600×1200 with a 5% safe margin. A user-specified aspect ratio overrides this default. Use portrait 4:5 for vertical feeds and square 1:1 for compact summaries.

## Selection rules

- Named model with 3–5 components → Framework explainer.
- Ordered actions or method → Step-by-step method.
- Two alternatives, misconception/correction, before/after → Comparison card.
- One central idea with independent consequences → Concept map.
- Article with claims, evidence, and application → Book or article note.
- Cause-and-effect transformation across time → Causal journey.
- Question with conditions and alternative choices → Decision path.

Describe the chosen reading path in the prompt. Do not use equal-width columns unless the content contains parallel categories.

## A. Framework explainer

Use for named models, equations, and 3–5 component frameworks.

1. Top 26%: title and short explanation on the left; mascot holding a board with the formula or thesis on the right.
2. Middle 34%: three or four equal columns, one component per column, each with a different informative mascot pose.
3. Bottom 32%: chart/relationship on the left and applied example/process on the right.
4. Footer 8%: lightbulb icon plus one memory sentence.

## B. Step-by-step method

Use for habits, procedures, tutorials, and playbooks.

1. Hero strip with title, promise, and mascot pointing toward the first step.
2. Three to five numbered steps connected by restrained arrows.
3. Each step contains one verb-led heading, one small scene, and at most two lines of explanation.
4. End with a checklist or “start today” action box.

## C. Comparison card

Use for A/B distinctions, misconceptions, before/after, or good/bad patterns.

1. Top thesis spanning the width.
2. Two balanced columns with identical information order.
3. Use green check and coral-red cross only as small semantic markers; keep yellow as the main brand accent.
4. Bottom synthesis row explains when to choose each side or how to move from one to the other.

## D. Concept map

Use for one central idea with related principles or consequences.

1. Central mascot and core concept in a rounded card.
2. Four satellite modules connected with thin hand-drawn arrows.
3. Put examples on the lower half and abstract principles on the upper half.
4. Finish with a one-line causal chain.

## E. Book or article note

Use for summarizing source material without flattening it into a generic framework.

1. Title, source, and one-sentence thesis at top.
2. Three key claims in stacked bands with small mascot scenes.
3. One “how to use it” panel with a concrete scenario.
4. Footer contains the reader's action, not a generic inspirational quote.

## F. Causal journey

Use for transformations, feedback loops, compounding effects, and arguments that unfold over time.

1. Start with a dominant initial-state scene.
2. Connect 4–6 uneven milestones with a rising, winding, or circular path.
3. Change mascot expression and posture at each milestone to show discovery, friction, adjustment, and result.
4. End with the causal conclusion and one next action.

## G. Decision path

Use for diagnostics, choices, conditional advice, and risk checks.

1. Put the decision question in a dominant top or central node.
2. Branch into two or three labeled paths with distinct outcomes.
3. Show the mascot observing, comparing, then deciding rather than repeating one pointing pose.
4. Merge the paths into a final rule, checklist, or action box.

## Variation within a pattern

- Alternate dominant zones among left hero, right hero, central focus, top banner, diagonal journey, and bottom synthesis.
- Mix one large panel with smaller supporting scenes when hierarchy matters; use equal panels only for parallel concepts.
- Place the mascot where the action occurs. Avoid repeating the same corner, scale, expression, or gesture across cards.

## Density limits

- 1:1: 40–90 Chinese characters of visible body copy.
- 4:3: 90–180 Chinese characters of visible body copy.
- 4:5: 80–160 Chinese characters of visible body copy.
- 3:4 or A4: 120–220 Chinese characters of visible body copy.
- If the draft exceeds the limit, split it into a series rather than shrinking typography.
